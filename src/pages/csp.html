The algebra underneath

Communicating Sequential Processes is a mathematical theory of concurrent systems. It is why occam programs can be proved correct and why their designs can be checked automatically before any code runs.

A CSP primer

In CSP a process is described by the events it can take part in. An event happens only when every process that has it in its alphabet agrees to it. That is the synchronisation behind occam's channels.

a → Ptake part in a, then behave like P (prefix)
c!v → P, c?x → P(x)output v on channel c; input a value and bind it to x
P □ Qexternal choice: the environment chooses (occam ALT)
P ⊓ Qinternal choice: the process chooses nondeterministically
P ‖A Qparallel, synchronising on the events in A (occam PAR)
P \ Ahiding: the events in A become internal (occam's local channel declaration)

A one-place buffer is COPY = left?x → right!x → COPY. The occam version is almost a transliteration:

PROC copy (CHAN INT left?, right!)
  WHILE TRUE
    INT x:
    SEQ
      left ? x
      right ! x
:

Semantic models

CSP has a hierarchy of denotational models. Each one records a different amount of what can be observed about a process:

  • Traces (T): the finite sequences of events a process can perform. Enough for safety properties ("nothing bad happens").
  • Stable failures (F): traces paired with the sets of events the process can refuse afterwards. Enough to capture deadlock and to tell external choice apart from internal choice.
  • Failures-divergences (FD): adds the traces after which a process can perform internal actions forever (livelock). This is the standard model for proving that a system is live.

Brookes, Hoare and Roscoe introduced the failures model in 1984. Roscoe's books develop the full hierarchy, including infinite-trace models and the algebraic and operational semantics that match the denotational ones.

Refinement

Specification and implementation are written in the same language. Spec ⊑ Impl ("Impl refines Spec") means every behaviour of the implementation is allowed by the specification in the chosen model. Refinement is compositional: refining one component of a parallel network refines the whole network. You can therefore design top-down, replacing an abstract process with a network of simpler ones and proving each step. That is the same nesting you see in folds and process diagrams.

Model checking with FDR

FDR (Failures-Divergences Refinement) is the standard refinement checker for CSP. It was first developed by Formal Systems (Europe) Ltd. FDR2 was the version the occam and CPA community used for years. FDR3 was a rewrite at Oxford (TACAS 2014). The current version, FDR4, is maintained by Cocotec and is free for academic use.

FDR reads CSPM, a machine-readable dialect of CSP with a functional expression language. Here two one-place buffers joined by a hidden channel are checked against the specification of a two-place buffer:

All three assertions pass. If you change COPY to swallow a value (say a?x -> COPY(a, b)), FDR reports a counterexample trace. It is the shortest sequence of events that breaks the specification, and it is often the fastest way to understand a concurrency bug.

This is how FDR is typically used on occam designs. Peter Welch and Jeremy Martin modelled Java's monitor primitives (synchronized, wait, notify) in CSP. They then used FDR to show that JCSP's implementations of channels and the alternative construct, built from those primitives, refine their CSP specifications. The result was a provably correct CSP library for Java (CPA 2000). FDR has also been used to verify occam-π barriers, protocol stacks and deadlock-freedom design rules such as client-server and I/O-par.

From occam to CSP

Each occam construct has a CSP meaning, and The Laws of occam Programming (Roscoe & Hoare, 1988) gives the algebra directly on occam syntax. Some examples:

Laws like these are sound in the failures-divergences model and complete for the finite language. They justified the program transformations used in the Inmos compiler, and they remain a good example of a programming language designed together with its semantics.
